## 🛰️ The problem

Most of the machines we sent into space never came home. Surveyor 3 still sits on the Moon. Spirit is parked in a sand trap on Mars. The Voyagers are still flying, 49 years later.

But if you are 12 and want their stories, you end up in catalogues, PDFs and technical archives. Most people only ever hear about the missions flying today, so the landers, rovers and probes that opened up the Solar System are, for most of us, already forgotten.

## ✨ Our answer

**Since 1958** puts every one of them in one place, on one clock.

Press **Start**. The Solar System runs from 1958 to today, with the Sun, the planets and the Moon where they really were on every date. Each machine arrives on the day it really arrived, at its real landing site. Then you can fly down to it, watch it land, walk up to it, take it apart, and learn **why it was sent, what it did, what we learned, why it stayed, and where it is now.**

No install, no account, no plug-in. It runs in any modern browser, on a laptop or a phone. A short hands-on tutorial teaches the controls by using them.

## 🧭 A 3-minute tour for judges

1. **Open the site and press Start.** A 40-second opening film falls to Tranquility Base, then pulls back across the Solar System. A light comes on for every machine left behind, then most of them turn grey: they are silent.
2. **Press ▶ on the clock.** Time runs from 1958. When a machine arrives, the view flies there and plays its arrival scene.
3. **Click the Moon → Apollo 11 → "Fire a laser at its mirror".** A pulse leaves Earth, hits the reflector the crew left in 1969, and comes back at the real speed of light, over today's real Earth–Moon distance.
4. **Open "Featured animations" → Apollo 11.** The whole flight in 14 scenes, from the pad to "The Eagle has landed".
5. **Press "Exhibition".** Walk a clean-room hall with 44 exhibits at true size, and take any of them apart piece by piece.
6. **Ride Voyager 1 and write it a message.** Your message travels out at the speed of light, and you can follow it.

> 💡 **Tab** skips any film. **H** hides every button. **Menu → What is real?** shows what is data, what is simplified, and what is illustration.

## 📡 NASA data at the core

NASA data isn't decoration here. The whole simulation is built on it.

| Source | Used for |
|---|---|
| **NASA NSSDCA Master Catalog** | Launch, landing and end dates; landing coordinates |
| **NASA Science mission pages** | The story chapters and facts on every object (each object links its own sources) |
| **NASA/JPL rover traverse maps** | The real routes of Curiosity, Perseverance and Ingenuity |
| **NASA 3D Resources** | Spacecraft models: lunar module, Viking, Spirit/Opportunity, Curiosity, Perseverance, Ingenuity, InSight, Hubble, Webb, Cassini and more |
| **NASA SVS CGI Moon Kit (LRO)** | The detailed Moon |
| **NASA SVS star maps** | About 150,000 real stars at their real places (Yale, Hipparcos, Tycho catalogues) |
| **Apollo Lunar Surface Journal & mission reports** | Flight sequences and timings in the Apollo 11 animation |
| **NASA Goddard / JPL lunar laser ranging** | The laser echo: round trip, mirror size, beam width |
| **NASA/JPL Voyager** | Voyager 1's real distance (one light-day from Earth on 18 November 2026) and the 2017 message campaign |

Planet and Moon positions for any date are computed with **Astronomy Engine**.

## ✅ Accuracy, honestly

A story about real machines has to be true, so we kept data and decoration apart:

- **Real data:** where the Sun, planets and Moon are on every date, at true size and distance; every landing and impact site; rover routes; dates and facts; NASA's 3D models; the stars.
- **Simplified:** probe flight paths between planets (each probe is at the right planet on the right day); hand-built models where NASA has none (Surveyor, the lunar rover, the Apollo science stations, Pathfinder/Sojourner, Phoenix, New Horizons, Voyager), all built to NASA's published sizes.
- **Illustration:** the fine detail of the Milky Way, flames and smoke in the films, and the rocks scattered around each site.

All of this is in the **"What is real?"** panel inside the site, so a student always knows what they are looking at.

## 🛠️ How it's built

| Layer | Tools |
|---|---|
| 3D engine | [three.js](https://threejs.org), custom **GLSL shaders** (planet lighting and atmospheres, Earth's night lights, earthshine on the Moon, eclipses) |
| Astronomy | [Astronomy Engine](https://github.com/cosinekitty/astronomy) for planet and Moon positions on any date |
| App | Plain **JavaScript (ES modules)**, HTML and CSS. No framework, no build step |
| Data prep | **Python** and **Blender** to prepare NASA data and models |
| Hosting | **GitHub Pages**, a static site with nothing to install |

Design choices for usability:
- Works on **phones** too: a touch joystick, a jump button and a compact layout.
- Adapts its resolution to keep a smooth frame rate on ordinary laptops.
- Respects **reduced-motion** settings, has ARIA labels, and works from the keyboard (**Tab** to skip, **H** to hide the UI, **Space** to jump in the exhibition).

### Run it yourself
```bash
git clone https://github.com/guembitangerine314-eng/since-1958.git
cd since-1958
python3 -m http.server 8000
# open http://localhost:8000  (ES modules do not load from file://)
```

### Controls
| Action | How |
|---|---|
| Look around / zoom | Drag / scroll (or touch) |
| Go to a world, site or probe | Click it, or use **Go to…** and **Find** |
| Control time | Clock panel (top right) or the timeline |
| Skip a film or animation | **Tab** |
| Exhibition | **W A S D** walk · **Space** jump · **V** 3rd person · **E** take apart |
| Hide everything but the view | **H** |
